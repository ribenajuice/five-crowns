Criteria 199–202, open question 10

Nemesis: five candidates, side by side

Open question 10 asked to see options, not have the team pick blind. All five below use the identical, real numbers — Player E finishes above Player C in 6 of their 9 shared games (66.7%) — so only the title and the framing sentence change. Each one passes the mechanical test in criterion 202: printable with both Player C and Player E reading it over each shoulder. None calls either player weak, dominated, owned, hopeless or a walkover; none says a player can't or never will win; none tells anyone what to do about it.

1 · Nemesis
Nemesis

Player E

Finishes above you in 6 of your 9 games together (66.7%).

The founder's own word, from the analytics catalogue — kept completely flat. The safe default if none of the banter options feel right for this screen.

2 · The upper hand
The upper hand

Player E has it

Above you in 6 of your 9 games together (66.7%).

Names what the number actually is — one player getting the better of another on a given night — without characterising either of them. Mild banter.

3 · The regular
The regular

Player E

Finishes above you more often than anyone else you've played — 6 of 9 games (66.7%).

Dry, understated — reads like a running joke at the table rather than a verdict about the games. Closest in tone to the rest of the board's titles.

4 · Bogeyman
Bogeyman

Player E

The one who finishes above you most often — 6 of your 9 games together (66.7%).

Leans furthest into banter of the five — playful, a little theatrical. Still strictly about the number, but it's the one we'd flag as worth a second look if you want the tone kept gentler.

5 · Frequent flyer
Frequent flyer

Player E

Finishes above you more than anyone else — 6 of 9 games together (66.7%).

A lighter pun on "flying above" you in the standings — banter with no edge at all. Closest to a safe, everyone-laughs choice.

Not shown as a sixth card, but worth knowing: where nobody has ever finished above a player (criterion 201), the card reuses the board's own no-holder sentence verbatim — "Nobody's done this yet." — rather than new copy. That part isn't in question; only the five titles above are.
