The four personality stats: card wording, for sign-off

Four new cards join the honours board's existing thirteen, taking it to seventeen. Every definition, number and drill-through is already locked in the PRD — nothing about what's counted is a design decision. What's below is only the wording criterion 312 reserved for this checkpoint: each card's title, its sample statement, and its no-holder sentence. Three real candidates per card, not one — same process as the nemesis card (M3 Stage 2), same mechanical test.

What's already settled and not up for debate here. Criterion 202 ("nothing characterises a player") is set aside in full for these four cards — titles and every sentence — per the founder's 2026-09-15 decision, going further than the team recommended. Two things stay absolute regardless: criterion 192's bans (never checked/validated/verified/ confirmed/correct, never safe/protected/self-cancelling) and the honesty rule (no invented, rounded or exaggerated number; no probability, no "expected", no claim about why). And criterion 202's own mechanical test still applies to every candidate below: printable with the named player reading it over your shoulder. A pointed title clears that bar. Telling somebody what to do about their number does not — none of the eleven candidates below do that.
Open question 12 stays open. Whether the board is still readable in five seconds at seventeen cards is explicitly deferred to the founder (criterion 296) — answering it is not this slice's job and I haven't tried to. My visual read is at the bottom, flagged, not resolved.

Open question 12 — flagged, not decided here

My visual read on seventeen cards

Not my call to make, and I haven't tried to. What I can report from looking at the frames above:

At 375px, seventeen stacked cards is a genuinely long scroll — roughly 3,000–3,500px, call it 10–14 screens' worth of thumb-scrolling depending on phone. Twelve cards already put this question on the table (Stage 3's ADR); seventeen is not a marginal case of the same worry, it's past the point where "readable in five seconds" and "the landing screen" can both be true of the whole board at once. What still is true at five seconds: the first 3–4 cards (whichever the group's most recent night made move) and the archive line at the top. The rest is a board you scroll and read, not glance at — which may be exactly fine for this app's audience of six friends, just worth naming plainly rather than leaving implicit.
No layout change was needed to fit seventeen cards — that part of your question has a clean answer: yes, as-is. If you do want to revisit five-second legibility later, the two cheapest levers, unexplored here because it's your call and not blocking this slice, are reordering (move the four newest, most personality-driven cards to the top, since they're the ones with the most "water-cooler" pull) or a plain subheading between groups of cards (no new component, one line of text) — not a rebuild either way.
